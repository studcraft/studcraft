# Glossary

**Version:** 0.2.0 Draft

---

## AP

Action Points.

The resource every unit spends during its activation. See `02-core-rules.md`, CORE-006.

---

## UB

Unit Base.

The universal measurement of StudCraft: a volume 4 studs wide, 3 studs deep and 13 plate layers tall. A footprint written `W × D UB` counts widths by depths; a distance written `N UB` counts N Unit Bases along the axis its rule names. See `02-core-rules.md` (CORE-001).

---

## Delivery Method

How an attack reaches its target: Line of Sight + Range for ranged weapons, or Physical Contact for melee weapons. The only point where ranged and melee combat differ — everything from Attack Dice generation onward is identical. See `11-combat.md`, `12-melee.md` (MEL-001, MEL-014).

---

## Weapon System

Each independently mounted or wielded weapon, resolving attacks independently of any other weapon system on the same platform. See `10-weapons.md` (WPN-008), `11-combat.md` (CBT-006).

---

## Attack Dice

The dice a weapon generates per attack: one per functional muzzle for ranged weapons, or one per independently wielded weapon for melee (regardless of striking-end count). See `10-weapons.md` (WPN-006), `12-melee.md` (MEL-003).

---

## Attack Roll

The die roll that determines whether a generated Attack Die becomes a successful Impact: 4, 5, or 6 succeeds, 1, 2, or 3 fails. Where the component providing the attack is Wounded, the roll is two dice with only the lower read, against that same threshold (`11-combat.md`, CBT-015). See `11-combat.md` (CBT-005).

---

## Impact

A successful attack result generated by a weapon. See `11-combat.md`, CBT-005.

Targets resolve Impacts using the Component Damage System (`16-damage-system.md`, DMG-008).

---

## Component

A single visible physical part of a model that combat targets independently (Pilot, Door, Wheel, Weapon, Track, and similar). Components have no Hit Points — only a Component State and a Resistance, both read from the model. See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-001, DMG-002, DMG-003).

---

## Priority

The right, held by the player in first place when the Activation Order is determined, to choose whether to activate first or to move to the last place instead. See `03-game-flow.md`, FLOW-003.

---

## Activation

The period during which one unit receives its Action Points (`02-core-rules.md`, CORE-006) and performs actions. The procedure is `03-game-flow.md`, FLOW-004.

---

## Open Transport

Passengers are visible.

They may attack and be attacked, subject to normal Line of Sight. See `09-transport.md`, TRN-009.

---

## Closed Transport

Passengers cannot be targeted directly; they are internal components the hull protects, reachable only if an Impact penetrates the hull with remaining strength (`09-transport.md`, TRN-010; `16-damage-system.md`, DMG-006, DMG-016).

---

## Functional Component

A LEGO element that has gameplay effects.

Decorative elements have none unless another rule gives them one. See `05-construction-components.md`, CMP-001.

---

## Facing

The direction a unit is oriented toward, and what movement is performed relative to — see `07-movement.md`, MOVE-001. Which part of a model is its front is its own domain's rule — see `02-core-rules.md`, CORE-002. Which way a shield protects is settled by where it physically stands rather than by Facing — see `05-construction-components.md`, CMP-014.

---

## Deployment Volume

The limit on a player's army, agreed before the game and measured in Unit Bases: a floor of `W × D` Unit Bases every model must be placed within, and a ceiling `H` every model must stand under. See `06-deployment.md` (DEP-001).

---

## Weapon Range

The maximum distance a ranged weapon may attack, equal to Weapon Length × 6. Bounded in practice by Line of Sight rather than by a written maximum. See `10-weapons.md` (WPN-005).

---

## Weapon Reach

Not a value looked up separately — a description of why a melee weapon's Physical Contact check (MEL-001) comes out the way it does. A longer Weapon Length is why a longer weapon reaches farther; there is no reach number to consult. See `12-melee.md` (MEL-014).

---

## Turn

A full round in which every unit on the battlefield is activated once. See `03-game-flow.md`, FLOW-002.

---

## Weapon Body

The functional physical structure of a weapon — barrel, mechanism and support — excluding mounting hardware and decoration. Defines Weapon Length (measured along the firing axis) and Weapon Width. See `10-weapons.md`, WPN-001, WPN-003, WPN-018.

---

## Weapon Front

The single face of a weapon from which it may fire. Muzzles may not be placed on any other face. See `10-weapons.md`, WPN-019.

---

## Weapon Front Footprint

The square construction area (Weapon Width × Weapon Width) on the Weapon Front available for muzzle placement. See `10-weapons.md`, WPN-019.

---

## Muzzle

A round functional construction area (typically 1×1 through 4×4, with no fixed maximum — see `10-weapons.md` WPN-002) on the Weapon Front Footprint, representing one firing barrel.

Each functional muzzle grants one Attack Die. Muzzles may be placed directly adjacent to one another.

---

## Muzzle Size

The size of a functional muzzle. No fixed maximum — scales with the Weapon Front Footprint (`10-weapons.md`, WPN-019, WPN-021). Determines the Impact Strength of the Attack Die it generates (size × 3).

---

## Functional Striking End

The melee equivalent of a muzzle: the physical contact surface through which a melee weapon delivers an Impact. Sized like a muzzle (no fixed maximum), but not required to be round. See `12-melee.md` (MEL-013).

---

## Impact Strength

The strength value carried by an Attack Die, equal to the size of the muzzle or functional striking end that generated it, multiplied by 3 (one stud of muzzle width = one brick of penetrating power = 3 plate layers). Expressed in the same unit as Resistance, so the Geometry Check compares like with like. See `10-weapons.md` (WPN-021).

---

## Weapon Capacity

The limit on total mounted Weapon Length a platform may carry, equal to its Platform Length. See `10-weapons.md`, WPN-004.

---

## Platform Length

The largest horizontal dimension of a platform's Unit Base or vehicle bounding box. The Unit Base's height is not a candidate. See `10-weapons.md` (WPN-004).

---

## Cargo Bay

The interior space of a transport vehicle, measured in Unit Bases, used to carry passengers and cargo. A closed bay with less than one Unit Base of clear height carries cargo but no infantry. See `09-transport.md` (TRN-019).

---

## Gameplay Geometry

The measurable properties of a model that feed a rule's measured value (Range, Attack Dice, Impact Strength, and similar). See `15-geometry-layers.md` (GEO-001).

---

## Visual Geometry

Every decorative element of a model that does not modify Gameplay Geometry. Never affects measured rule values; still counts for physical checks like Line of Sight and Cover, and for the Resistance of material an Impact crosses. See `15-geometry-layers.md` (GEO-002, GEO-004).

---

## Functional Equivalence

Two models with identical Gameplay Geometry produce identical measured values, regardless of differences in Visual Geometry. See `15-geometry-layers.md` (GEO-005).

---

## Resistance

The smallest cross-section of material an Impact must cross in its direction of travel, measured in plate layers and read directly from the model, not assigned as a statistic — no component type is exempt, moulded pieces included. See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-003, DMG-004).

---

## Terrain Threshold

The height a vehicle can cross or descend without being blocked or stranded, read from its locomotion rather than assigned: axle height for wheels and tracks, knee height for walkers, hover assembly height for hover. In every case you measure the part that carries the vehicle, not the body it carries. Measured in plate layers. See `08-vehicles.md` (VEH-021 through VEH-024).

---

## Stranded

A vehicle that has entered a drop deeper than its Terrain Threshold. A stranded vehicle is Immobilized (`08-vehicles.md`, VEH-019, VEH-025) and keeps operating its remaining systems. Hover vehicles cannot be stranded.

---

## Armour

Not a separate statistic — the colloquial name for a component's construction-derived Resistance. See `11-combat.md` (CBT-013), `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-003, DMG-004).

---

## Component State

The three-state progression every component uses: `Operational`, `Wounded`, `Dead`. No component type is exempt from the states; only a Wounded minifigure can run them backwards (`16-damage-system.md`, DMG-018). See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-002).

---

## Geometry Check

The step in damage resolution that compares an Impact's Strength against a component's Resistance; below Resistance, the Impact ends immediately with no further roll. See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-013).

---

## Damage Roll

The die roll that follows a passed Geometry Check; on a 1, 2, or 3 the component's state advances by one step. See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-014).

---

## Penetration

Also called Overrun. When an Impact's strength exceeds a component's Resistance, the remaining strength (`Impact Strength − Component Resistance`) continues toward the next component behind it. See `16-damage-system.md` (DMG-016).

---

## Scenario

The definition of one particular game: how it ends and how victory is judged, at minimum, and optionally the battlefield, the terrain and any restriction on otherwise-legal actions. The ruleset defines how models behave; the scenario defines what the players are trying to do. See `03-game-flow.md` (FLOW-013).

---

## Access Opening

The gap a model passes through when it uses a door, hatch or ramp. See `05-construction-components.md` (CMP-018).

---

## Interior Clearance

The clear height inside a closed compartment, measured in plate layers from the surface the model rests on — for infantry, the surface under its base rather than the top of it. Less than one Unit Base of clear height carries cargo but no infantry. See `09-transport.md` (TRN-019).

---

## Slice

The share of a Unit Base one cargo object occupies: 4 × 3 studs by that object's own height. Slices sharing a Unit Base may total no more than the Unit Base's height, and infantry never occupies a slice — it occupies the whole Unit Base. See `09-transport.md` (TRN-013).

---

## Interactive Element

Any LEGO element that can physically move and be operated during play — a door, hatch, ramp, drawbridge, elevator or gate. It must physically exist on the model rather than be declared, and operating one costs 1 Action Point unless a rule says otherwise. See `02-core-rules.md`, CORE-007 and `05-construction-components.md`, CMP-019.

---

## Activation Order

The order in which players activate their units through a Turn: every player rolls 1D6 at its start, and the order runs from the highest result to the lowest. A player who cedes Priority moves to its last place. A player with no units left on the battlefield takes no place in it at all. See `03-game-flow.md`, FLOW-003.

---

## Wounded

The middle Component State: the component still functions, and where the capability it provides is one of the three the ruleset degrades, it functions worse — a Wounded infantry model's movement (`17-infantry.md`, INF-012), the movement of a vehicle whose Pilot is Wounded (`08-vehicles.md`, VEH-031), and how each Attack Die is read when the component providing the attack is Wounded, whether that is the weapon or an unarmed attacker (`11-combat.md`, CBT-015). A component that provides none of those — a door, a wheel, a shield — loses nothing by being Wounded. Nothing else about any component changes, and the next successful damaging Impact advances it to Dead. A Wounded infantry model is placed seated, which is how the state is shown on the model. See `16-damage-system.md`, DMG-002.

---

## Unit

What activates and receives Action Points: infantry or a vehicle. A structure is not a unit. A model a vehicle carries is a unit of its own. See `02-core-rules.md`, CORE-003, CORE-004, CORE-005, CORE-006, and `09-transport.md`, TRN-021.

---
