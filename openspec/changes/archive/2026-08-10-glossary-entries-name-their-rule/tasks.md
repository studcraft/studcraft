# Tasks — Every glossary entry names the rule that owns it

## How to apply this change

Every anchor below was checked with `python3 scripts/check_task_anchors.py
glossary-entries-name-their-rule` against the pre-change files and occurs **exactly once
in the file its task names**. Replace the whole anchor.

If an anchor returns anything other than 1, **stop and report it** rather than guessing
which occurrence was meant. Never edit a document to make a verification command pass —
report the mismatch instead.

### How to read the replacement blocks

The triple-backtick fence marks where the text starts and stops. **The fence is not part
of the text** — do not write the backticks into the document.

Each glossary anchor is a whole entry, from its `## Term` heading through its last line of
text. The `---` separator that follows each entry is **outside** every anchor and is never
touched.

The backticks *inside* a block — around filenames such as `` `03-game-flow.md` `` — **are**
part of the text and must be written into the document.

### What this change does and does not do

**Eleven entries gain a `See …` sentence appended to their last paragraph, with no other
wording changed.** Those are tasks 1.1 – 1.11.

**Two edits are not that shape, and both are deliberate.** An earlier draft of this file
claimed in three places that no existing wording is ever altered; that was false of its
own task 1.2, and the claim has been corrected rather than the tasks:

- **Task 1.2 (`Impact`) edits an existing sentence.** Its `See …` sentence goes at the end
  of the **first** paragraph, and the parenthetical citation already in its last paragraph
  gains a rule ID. Both halves are given verbatim below.
- **Task 2.1 rewrites a rule heading and adds one sentence to that rule's body.**

No rule ID is added, removed or renumbered anywhere. `SCS-006` keeps its number; only the
words after its em dash change.

## 2. The `Functional Components` title collision

things. `design.md` records why `SCS-006` is the one that changes: its own first line reads
*"Interactive elements must physically exist"*, so its title contradicts its body.

The same task adds a pointer to `CORE-007`. `SCS-006` and `CORE-007` list the same six
examples and neither cites the other; `SCS-007` and `SCS-008`, `SCS-006`'s immediate
neighbours, both cite `CORE-007` correctly. Retitling `SCS-006` to the words that head
`CORE-007`'s own section without connecting the two would move a duplication rather than
resolve it.

## 5. Repairs found by the audit of the applied text

`ruleset-auditor` read the finished documents after sections 1 to 4 landed and raised
seven items. Six are repaired here; the seventh is recorded as untouched.

**Five of the six are the same defect class, and the change created it.** An entry that
cites no rule cannot contradict one. Adding the citation is exactly what made these
visible: five entries state *less* than the rule they now name, or state it differently,
and a reader following the new citation arrives somewhere that does not match.

`design.md` said no entry's definition would be corrected. That was right for a definition
that is merely imprecise, and wrong for one that contradicts the rule this change points it
at — the citation is what turns the second into a defect of this change rather than a
pre-existing one.

- [x] 5.1 `Weapon Body` — replaced "The continuous physical structure of a weapon" with
  "The functional physical structure of a weapon — barrel, mechanism and support —
  excluding mounting hardware and decoration". No rule requires a weapon body to be
  *continuous*; the word appears nowhere else in `docs/`. Both `SCS-017` ("Decoration does
  not count") and `WPN-003` ("Mounting hardware and decorative elements are not part of it
  and are ignored") carry the exclusion the entry omitted. A reader measuring all the
  plastic gets Weapon Length wrong, which propagates into `WPN-004` and `WPN-005`.

- [x] 5.2 `Priority` — was "The player who chooses…", now "The right to choose…, held by
  the player who rolled highest and cedable to the opponent". `FLOW-003` treats Priority as
  a status gained and ceded, not as a person; under the old wording "Cede Priority" reads
  as ceding a player.

- [x] 5.3 `Facing` — the "Determines" list named two of the seven consequences `CORE-002`
  lists, in a form that reads exhaustive. It now names all seven, including shield
  direction, which `SCS-022` leans on.

- [x] 5.4 `Open Transport` — "They may attack and be attacked" now ends "subject to normal
  Line of Sight". `TRN-009` requires it; the entry read as unconditional.

- [x] 5.5 `SCS-006`'s new pointer said "What may become one, **and** what operating it
  costs, are defined in CORE-007" — and was immediately followed by the six examples of
  what may become one. The sentence delegated the example set and then reprinted it. It now
  reads "What operating one costs is defined in `02-core-rules.md`, CORE-007", which is the
  half `SCS-006` genuinely does not carry.

- [x] 5.6 Added a glossary entry for **`Interactive Element`**, citing `CORE-007` and
  `SCS-006`. The retitle made this a rule title, a section heading and the term four rules
  already use — the most prominent term this change touched, and the only one left without
  an entry while its former namesake `Functional Component` gained one.

**Deliberately not repaired.** `CMP-009` opens "Doors are interactive components", which is
neither `Interactive Element` nor `Functional Component`. It is pre-existing drift that the
retitle makes visible rather than causes, and correcting a rule's wording is a change of
its own. Also untouched: the `Muzzle` entry's `` see `10-weapons.md` WPN-002 `` citation
form, which uses neither the comma nor the parenthesised convention. The linter accepts it,
and it predates this change.
